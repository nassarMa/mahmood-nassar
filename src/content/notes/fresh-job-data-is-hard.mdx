When I started working on career intelligence, I assumed the hard part would
be scraping. Careers pages are inconsistent, some render with JavaScript, some
block bots. Annoying, but solvable.

The hard part turned out to be a question, not a page: **what does "fresh"
mean?**

## Three things that look like new roles and are not

1. A role reposted with a new ID because the recruiter refreshed it.
2. The same role listed on the company page, two boards and an aggregator,
   each with slightly different titles.
3. A role that is technically open but has been "open" for eleven months.

If you count any of these as new, the person on the other end gets a
notification that wastes their time, and after three of those they stop
trusting the system.

## What actually works

- Go to the source. The company's own careers page is the canonical record;
  boards are echoes of it.
- Model identity, not URLs. A role is a company, a title family, a location
  and a rough seniority. Two listings that match on those are one role.
- Track state over time. New is only meaningful relative to what was there
  yesterday. Closed is only knowable because something disappeared.
- Let an agent do the last check. When the pipeline is unsure whether a role
  is real or still open, an agent can read the page the way a careful person
  would and say so.

The scraping still matters. But the value is in the model of freshness that
sits on top of it, and that is where most of the thinking goes.
