A restaurant owner takes a photo of their menu, adds a name, a phone number and a few photos, and gets a published website with its own address. The builder works in Arabic, English and Hebrew, and the website language can differ from the owner's.

**How it was built.** Claude reads the menu photo into structured dishes and prices, then picks a design direction, lays out the page around the owner's photos and writes the copy. The AI never writes code: it fills a validated configuration, and a fixed set of hand-built designs renders it. Before anything goes live, a quality check compares every price on the page with the price read from the menu.

**What visitors get.** A calm, phone-first site with menu search, tap-to-call, directions, and an order list that customers can send to the restaurant on WhatsApp.

**What I learned.** Accuracy beats creativity on a menu: guests forgive a plain design, but never a wrong price. And design taste has to be written down and enforced, not hoped for: the first version looked generic until each design direction was hand-built and the AI was told to choose calm over loud.
